# Pricing-WTP Skill

This skill provides a structured approach to researching, testing, and optimizing pricing strategies that maximize revenue while remaining attractive to target customers. Unlike arbitrary pricing or cost-plus approaches, this skill emphasizes evidence-based pricing grounded in customer willingness to pay and validated value propositions.

## When to Use This Skill

- After value proposition development and validation
- Following pricing strategy ideation
- Before finalizing pricing for market launch
- When preparing to communicate pricing to customers, investors, or partners
- To create a shared understanding of why our price is justified

## Core Principles

1. **Start with customer value, not costs** - Focus on what customers are willing to pay, not what it costs us to build
2. **Test pricing with real customer behavior** - Measure actual payment decisions, not just stated intent
3. **Consider psychological pricing effects** - Recognize that prices influence perception beyond their numeric value
4. **Segment pricing when appropriate** - Different customer segments may have different willingness to pay
5. **Monitor and adjust based on market feedback** - Pricing is not a one-time decision

## Pricing Framework

Effective pricing strategies answer three fundamental questions:

### 1. What is the customer willing to pay? (WTP)
- Specific, validated, and segmented when appropriate
- Examples: "$30/month for small businesses," "$5 premium for power users," "$0.99 per transaction for occasional users"

### 2. How does our price compare to alternatives? (Competitive Context)
- What do customers currently pay for alternatives?
- How does our price compare in the market landscape?
- Examples: "20% below average for similar tools," "Premium pricing justified by 2x value," "Parity pricing with market leader"

### 3. What is our pricing strategy? (Revenue Model)
- How we structure pricing to maximize revenue and customer satisfaction
- Examples: "Tiered pricing (basic/pro/enterprise)," "Freemium with premium upgrades," "Transaction-based pricing with volume discounts"

## Pricing Statement Format

Our [revenue model] prices [product/service] at [price point(s)] based on [customer WTP] unlike [alternative pricing approach] by [unique pricing mechanism].

## Examples

### Strong Pricing Statements
- "Our tiered pricing model prices our accessibility auditing tool at $20/month (basic), $40/month (pro), and $80/month (enterprise) based on validated willingness to pay from small businesses unlike flat-rate pricing by capturing value across different business sizes."
- "Our freemium pricing model prices our fitness challenge app at $0 (free) with $4.99/month premium based on validated willingness to pay from casual users unlike subscription-only pricing by lowering barrier to entry while monetizing power users."
- "Our transaction-based pricing prices our reservation system at $0.50 per booking based on validated willingness to pay from restaurant owners unlike subscription pricing by aligning cost with actual usage and value received."

### Weak Pricing Statements (to avoid)
- "We price our tool at $19.99 because it's a nice round number." (Arbitrary, not customer-focused)
- "Our pricing is cost-plus 50% markup." (Cost-focused, not customer-value focused)
- "We chose this price because our competitors use it." (Competitor-focused, not customer-validation focused)

## Pricing-WTP Development Process

### 1. Preparation
- Gather value proposition and problem insights
- Review pricing ideas and alternatives
- Identify target customer and their validated willingness to pay
- Prepare pricing templates

### 2. WTP Discovery

#### Willingness to Pay Discovery
- What is the maximum customers would pay for our solution?
- What price points would customers consider fair or reasonable?
- How does willingness to pay vary across customer segments?

#### WTP Validation Methods
- **Price Sensitivity Tests**: Van Westendorp, Gabor-Granger, conjoint analysis
- **Purchase Intent Surveys**: Measuring stated likelihood to buy at different prices
- **Pre-order Campaigns**: Measuring actual purchase commitment at different price points
- **Landing Page Tests**: Measuring sign-ups or click-throughs at different prices
- **Actual Purchase Tests**: Measuring real payment behavior at different price points
- **Auction or Bidding Experiments**: Letting customers reveal WTP through bidding

#### WTP Segmentation
- Are there distinct customer segments with different WTP?
- Should we consider tiered, volume-based, or segmented pricing?
- What factors drive WTP differences (usage, needs, budget, etc.)?

### 3. Competitive Analysis

#### Alternative Pricing Research
- What do customers currently pay for direct alternatives?
- What pricing models do competitors use (subscription, freemium, transactional, etc.)?
- What are the price points and pricing structures in the market?

#### Competitive Positioning
- How does our price compare to alternatives in the market?
- What value justifications support our price relative to alternatives?
- What price sensitivity exists in the market for similar solutions?

### 4. Pricing Strategy Definition

#### Revenue Model Selection
- Choose between subscription, freemium, transactional, licensing, usage-based, etc.
- Consider predictability of revenue, customer preferences, and market norms
- Align revenue model with value delivery pattern and customer relationship

#### Price Point Determination
- Set specific price points based on WTP validation
- Consider psychological pricing effects (e.g., $29.99 vs $30.00)
- Ensure price points are profitable when combined with cost structure

#### Pricing Mechanism Definition
- Define how price varies with usage, features, customer type, etc.
- Consider discounts, bundles, promotions, and loyalty programs
- Ensure pricing mechanism is clear, consistent, and difficult to game

### 5. Pricing Testing

#### Comprehension Testing
- Do target customers understand our pricing?
- Do they understand what they get for their money?

#### Resonance Testing
- Does our pricing feel fair and reasonable to target customers?
- Does it align with their perception of value and fairness?

#### Credibility Testing
- Do target customers believe we can sustain our pricing?
- Does our pricing align with what they know about our costs and value?

#### Competitive Testing
- How does our pricing compare to alternatives in the eyes of customers?
- Would they choose our solution over alternatives based on pricing alone?

#### Behavioral Testing
- What actual payment behavior do we observe at our price points?
- Do customers actually pay what we expect them to pay?

### 6. Pricing Refinement

#### Iterative Improvement
- Test, learn, and refine based on customer feedback and behavior
- Try different pricing formulations to see what works best
- Consider A/B testing different price points or pricing models

#### Consistency Check
- Ensure pricing aligns with value proposition, problem insights, and cost structure
- Verify that claimed value justifications are actually supported by evidence

## Evidence Collection

- Record the target customer and their validated willingness to pay
- Document the pricing strategy, price points, and revenue model
- Capture the competitive analysis and pricing comparisons
- Record the exact pricing statement(s) tested
- Collect customer feedback on comprehension, resonance, credibility, competitiveness, and actual payment behavior

## Skill Overview

The pricing-WTP skill enables practitioners to research, test, and optimize pricing strategies that maximize revenue while remaining attractive to target customers. Unlike arbitrary pricing or cost-plus approaches, this approach emphasizes evidence-based pricing grounded in customer willingness to pay and validated value propositions, ensuring that the price reflects what customers actually value and are willing to pay for.

## Key Differentiators

- Focuses on starting with customer willingness to pay rather than costs
- Requires pricing strategies to be specific about target customer, WTP, and revenue model
- Emphasizes testing pricing with real customer payment behavior, not just stated intent
- Considers psychological pricing effects that influence perception beyond numeric value
- Demands testing with target customers before finalizing the pricing strategy

## Common Applications

- Developing pricing strategies for new ventures and products
- Refining pricing strategies based on customer feedback and validation
- Creating investor-ready pricing statements
- Building sales and marketing materials around justified pricing
- Communicating why our price is justified based on customer value and validation

## Success Indicators

After applying this skill, you should be able to:
- Articulate pricing strategies that start with specific customer willingness to pay
- Identify specific, validated target customer willingness to pay
- Specify specific, actionable pricing strategies and revenue models
- Compare our pricing to alternatives in specific, relevant ways
- Test pricing strategies with target customers and refine based on feedback and behavior
- Use customer language rather than jargon in pricing strategies

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Starting with pricing strategy rather than customer willingness to pay
- Being vague about the target customer or their willingness to pay
- Failing to validate willingness to pay through customer payment behavior
- Creating undifferentiated pricing strategies that don't stand out in the market
- Using jargon, acronyms, or unclear language that hinders understanding
- Finalizing pricing strategies without testing with target customers

## Next Steps

After completing pricing-WTP development, consider:
- Creating offer designs that operationalize the pricing strategy and value proposition
- Developing financial models based on the pricing strategy and cost structure
- Building minimum viable products that customers will actually pay for
- Sharing pricing-WTP statements with the venture team and advisors
