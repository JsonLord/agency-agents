# Offer Design Skill

This skill provides a structured approach to designing and testing complete offers that combine product/service, pricing, positioning, and messaging for market validation. Unlike product-focused development or arbitrary bundling, this skill emphasizes evidence-based offer design grounded in customer problems, validated value propositions, and tested pricing strategies.

## When to Use This Skill

- After value proposition, positioning, and pricing validation
- Before investing significantly in full product development
- When preparing to launch market validation experiments
- To create a shared understanding of what we're offering to the market

## Core Principles

1. **Start with customer problems, not solution features** - Focus on what customers care about, not what we find cool to build
2. **Validate before building** - Test market interest with minimal viable offers before full development
3. **Ensure offer components work together cohesively** - Product, pricing, positioning, and messaging must align
4. **Test the complete offer, not just individual parts** - Validate the whole package customers will experience
5. **Consider both financial and non-financial value exchanges** - Money, time, data, effort, etc. all matter
6. **Validate with behaviors, not just stated intent** - Focus on what customers actually do, not what they say they'll do

## Offer Framework

A strong offer addresses four fundamental dimensions:

### 1. What are we offering? (Product/Service)
- Specific enough to be actionable
- Examples: "Accessibility auditing tool for web development," "Social fitness challenge mobile app," "Online restaurant reservation system"

### 2. How much does it cost? (Pricing)
- Specific price points, pricing structure, and revenue model
- Examples: "$20/month basic, $40/month pro, $80/month enterprise," "$0 free with $4.99/month premium," "$0.50 per transaction"

### 3. Why should they care? (Positioning)
- Specific target customer, problem solved, and key benefit
- Examples: "For small business owners struggling with costly late-stage accessibility redesigns," "For mobile app users struggling with staying motivated to exercise regularly," "For restaurant owners struggling with time-consuming manual reservation processes"

### 4. What is the message? (Messaging)
- Specific value proposition, differentiation, and call to action
- Examples: "Reduce accessibility fixing costs by 70% by catching issues early," "Increase workout consistency by 40% through peer accountability and rewards," "Save 5 hours per week on reservation management by automating confirmations and reminders"

## Offer Statement Format

We offer [product/service] at [price point(s)] for [target customer] who [struggle with/experience] [customer problem] to [key benefit] unlike [alternative] by [unique mechanism] because [proof points].

## Examples

### Strong Offer Statements
- "We offer an accessibility auditing tool for web development at $20/month basic, $40/month pro, $80/month enterprise for small business owners who struggle with costly late-stage accessibility redesigns to reduce fixing costs by 70% unlike manual audits by catching issues early in development because it integrates directly into CI/CD pipelines."
- "We offer a social fitness challenge mobile app at $0 free with $4.99/month premium for mobile app users who struggle with staying motivated to exercise regularly to increase workout consistency by 40% unlike solo tracking apps by combining peer accountability with rewarding progression because it uses proven behavioral science principles."
- "We offer an online restaurant reservation system at $0.50 per transaction for restaurant owners who struggle with time-consuming manual reservation processes to save 5 hours per week on reservation management unlike paper books by automating confirmations, reminders, and table management because it reduces no-shows by 30% through automated reminders."

### Weak Offer Statements (to avoid)
- "We offer a great tool with lots of features." (Too vague, no customer focus)
- "We built an app that people might like." (No customer problem or validation)
- "Our service is available for purchase at various price points." (No value proposition or positioning)

## Offer Design Process

### 1. Preparation
- Gather validated value proposition, positioning, and pricing
- Review problem insights and solution benefits
- Identify target customer, problem, solution, price, positioning, and messaging
- Prepare offer design templates

### 2. Problem-Solution Connection

#### Problem Validation
- Confirm the problem is real, painful, and worth solving
- Quantify problem frequency, intensity, and impact when possible
- Identify which aspects of the problem our solution addresses

#### Solution Benefits
- Identify what our solution actually does for the customer
- Determine which benefits are most valuable to the customer
- Validate benefits through prototyping, testing, or customer feedback

#### Alternative Analysis
- Identify what customers currently do to solve the problem
- Understand the strengths and weaknesses of current alternatives
- Determine how our solution is different and preferably better

### 3. Value Proposition Articulation

#### Value Proposition Development
- Craft and test value propositions that connect problem and solution
- Validate that the value proposition resonates with target customers

#### Value Proposition Refinement
- Refine value proposition based on customer feedback
- Ensure value proposition accurately reflects problem-solution connection

### 4. Positioning Definition

#### Positioning Development
- Define and test how we want to be perceived relative to alternatives
- Validate that the positioning is actually held in the target customer's mind

#### Positioning Refinement
- Refine positioning based on customer feedback
- Ensure positioning accurately reflects target customer, problem, and differentiation

### 5. Pricing Strategy Determination

#### Pricing-WTP Development
- Research, test, and optimize pricing strategies based on customer willingness to pay
- Validate that the pricing strategy is attractive to target customers

#### Pricing Refinement
- Refine pricing strategy based on customer feedback and behavior
- Ensure pricing strategy aligns with value proposition and cost structure

### 6. Message Development

#### Value Proposition Communication
- Craft and test messaging that communicates value proposition and differentiation
- Validate that messaging is clear, compelling, and credible to target customers

#### Message Refinement
- Refine messaging based on customer feedback
- Ensure messaging accurately reflects value proposition and differentiation

### 7. Offer Assembly

#### Component Integration
- Ensure product/service, pricing, positioning, and messaging work together cohesively
- Verify that claimed benefits, price, positioning, and messaging are all supported by evidence

#### Offer Validation
- Test the complete offer with target customers to validate market interest
- Consider concierge tests, fake door tests, or landing page tests for initial validation

### 8. Offer Refinement

#### Iterative Improvement
- Test, learn, and refine based on customer feedback and behavior
- Try different offer formulations to see what works best
- Consider A/B testing different offer elements (price, messaging, etc.)

#### Consistency Check
- Ensure the offer aligns with problem insights, solution benefits, value proposition, positioning, and pricing
- Verify that all claimed elements are actually supported by evidence

## Evidence Collection

- Record the target customer and problem being addressed
- Document the solution benefits and how they were validated
- Capture the alternatives analysis and differentiation points
- Record the value proposition and how it was developed
- Document the positioning statement and how it was defined
- Record the pricing strategy and how it was determined
- Collect customer feedback on offer comprehension, resonance, credibility, competitiveness, and actual behavior

## Skill Overview

The offer design skill enables practitioners to design and test complete offers that combine product/service, pricing, positioning, and messaging in a way that resonates with target customers and validates market interest. Unlike product-focused development or arbitrary bundling, this approach emphasizes evidence-based offer design grounded in customer problems, validated value propositions, and tested pricing strategies, ensuring that the offer actually addresses what customers care about and provides a compelling reason to choose our solution.

## Key Differentiators

- Focuses on starting with customer problems rather than solution features
- Requires offers to be specific about product/service, price, target customer, problem, benefit, and differentiation
- Demands that offer components (product, pricing, positioning, messaging) work together cohesively
- Mandates testing the complete offer with target customers, not just individual parts
- Requires validation with actual customer behavior, not just stated intent

## Common Applications

- Developing offers for new ventures and products
- Refining offers based on customer feedback and validation
- Creating investor-ready offer statements
- Building sales and marketing materials around validated offers
- Communicating why customers should choose our offer over alternatives

## Success Indicators

After applying this skill, you should be able to:
- Articulate offer statements that start with specific target customer and problem
- Identify specific, validated target customers and their problems
- Specify specific, measurable solution benefits tied to customer problems
- Define specific, actionable pricing strategies and revenue models
- Articulate specific, actionable positioning statements with differentiation
- Craft specific, actionable value propositions connecting problem and solution
- Test offer statements with target customers and refine based on feedback and behavior
- Use customer language rather than jargon in offer statements

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Starting with offer elements rather than customer problems
- Being vague about the target customer or their problem
- Failing to validate solution benefits through customer feedback
- Creating undifferentiated offers that don't stand out in the market
- Using jargon, acronyms, or unclear language that hinders understanding
- Finalizing offer statements without testing with target customers

## Next Steps

After completing offer design, consider:
- Creating financial models based on the offer and cost structure
- Developing minimum viable products that deliver the promised offer
- Building market validation experiments to test offer interest
- Sharing offer statements with the venture team and advisors
