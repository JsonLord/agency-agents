# Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) Skill

This skill provides a structured approach to understanding the jobs customers are trying to get done when they seek solutions, focusing on the progress they want to make in specific circumstances. Unlike product-centric approaches, JTBD focuses on the customer's goal and the hiring decision they make to achieve progress.

## When to Use This Skill

- After problem discovery and before solution ideation
- When developing value propositions and positioning
- To understand customer motivation and context
- Before pricing strategy development
- To identify opportunities for innovation and differentiation

## Core Principles

1. **Customers hire products to make progress** - Focus on the job the customer is trying to get done
2. **Context matters** - The circumstances under which the job arises are critical
3. **Jobs are stable over time** - While solutions change, the core job-to-be-done remains consistent
4. **Look beyond the product** - Understand what caused the customer to look for a solution
5. **Jobs have functional, social, and emotional dimensions** - Consider all aspects of the progress sought

## JTBD Framework

### 1. The Job Statement

A job-to-be-done statement has the format:
"When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]."

#### Situation
- The triggering circumstance or context
- What is happening in the customer's world when they realize they need to make progress?
- Examples: "When I receive a legal complaint about website accessibility,"
          "When I notice users abandoning my checkout flow,"
          "When my team spends hours fixing accessibility issues after launch"

#### Motivation
- What the customer wants to achieve or avoid
- The progress they are trying to make
- Examples: "I want to avoid costly late-stage redesigns,"
          "I want to increase conversion rates,"
          "I want to launch without accessibility issues"

#### Expected Outcome
- The desired end state or result
- What progress looks like when successfully achieved
- Examples: "so I can comply with regulations and avoid fines,"
          "so I can recover lost sales and improve user experience,"
          "so I can release on schedule with confidence"

### 2. Job Dimensions

#### Functional Job
- The core task or problem to be solved
- What the customer is trying to accomplish practically
- Examples: "Test website for accessibility issues,"
          "Improve checkout conversion,"
          "Fix accessibility problems before launch"

#### Social Job
- How the customer wants to be perceived by others
- The social status or image they want to achieve or avoid
- Examples: "Be seen as compliant and responsible,"
          "Be viewed as innovative and user-focused,"
          "Avoid being seen as negligent or outdated"

#### Emotional Job
- How the customer wants to feel or avoid feeling
- The emotional state they seek to achieve or avoid
- Examples: "Feel confident and secure,"
          "Feel proud of the product and team,"
          "Avoid stress, anxiety, or embarrassment"

### 3. JTBD Hierarchy

#### Life Core Goal
- The fundamental goal in a customer's life (e.g., "Be successful in my career")

#### Related JTBDs
- Jobs that contribute to the life core goal (e.g., "Deliver high-quality projects on time")

#### Target JTBD
- The specific job we are focusing on (e.g., "Ensure website accessibility before launch")

#### Sub-jobs
- Smaller jobs that make up the target JTBD (e.g., "Identify accessibility issues," "Prioritize fixes," "Verify remediation")

### 4. JTBD Triggers and Barriers

#### Triggers
- Events or circumstances that cause the customer to seek a solution
- Examples: Receiving a legal threat, launching a new feature, hearing customer complaints

#### Barriers
- Factors that prevent the customer from making progress or hiring a solution
- Examples: Lack of budget, perceived complexity, fear of disruption, uncertainty about effectiveness

### 5. JTBD Outcomes

#### Desired Outcomes
- What the customer hopes to achieve by getting the job done
- Can be functional, social, or emotional

#### Undesired Outcomes
- What the customer wants to avoid
- Negative consequences they want to prevent

#### Related Outcomes
- Other outcomes that may be affected by getting the job done

## JTBD Process

### 1. Preparation
- Clearly define the problem space and target customer
- Identify hypotheses about the jobs customers might be trying to get done
- Prepare JTBD templates

### 2. JTBD Discovery

#### Discovery Methods
- **Customer Interviews**: Focus on the timeline of events leading to looking for a solution
  - "Tell me about the last time you faced [problem]."
  - "What happened that made you decide to do something about it?"
  - "What were you hoping to achieve?"
- **Job Mapping**: Have customers map out the steps they take when trying to get the job done
- **Timeline Analysis**: Reconstruct the sequence of events from trigger to solution
- **Success Story Interview**: Focus on times when they successfully got the job done
- **Failure Story Interview**: Focus on times when they failed to get the job done

#### JTBD Signals
- Customers describe a situation, motivation, and expected outcome
- Language includes "when," "I want to," "so I can"
- Discussion of events leading up to looking for a solution
- Talk about what they hoped to achieve or avoid

### 3. JTBD Articulation

#### Writing Job Statements
- Use the "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]." format
- Ensure the situation is specific and observable
- Ensure the motivation is about progress, not just a problem
- Ensure the expected outcome describes the desired end state

#### Validating Job Statements
- Check that the job statement is stable across different customers
- Verify that the situation is a common trigger
- Confirm that the motivation and expected outcome are meaningful to customers

#### JTBD Documentation
- Record the exact job statement
- Note the customer or source
- Capture the context and circumstances
- Document any variations or related jobs

### 4. JTBD Analysis

#### Job Structure Analysis
- Break down the job into sub-jobs or steps
- Identify where customers struggle or get stuck
- Determine which parts of the job are most important or painful

#### Trigger Analysis
- Identify common events that cause customers to seek solutions
- Determine the strength and frequency of different triggers

#### Barrier Analysis
- Identify what prevents customers from getting the job done
- Determine which barriers are most significant

#### Outcome Analysis
- Understand what customers hope to achieve
- Identify what they want to avoid
- Determine which outcomes are most valued

### 5. JTBD Application

#### Opportunity Identification
- Identify where current solutions fall short in helping customers get the job done
- Find gaps in the job execution that represent innovation opportunities

#### Solution Development
- Design solutions that help customers get the job done better
- Focus on the whole job, not just isolated features
- Consider how the solution fits into the customer's workflow

#### Positioning and Messaging
- Frame the product in terms of the job it helps get done
- Use JTBD language in value propositions and positioning statements
- Address the situation, motivation, and expected outcome in messaging

#### Innovation and Differentiation
- Look for ways to help customers get the job done in new or better ways
- Consider job-related innovations that competitors overlook

## Evidence Collection

- Record job statements with customer identification and date
- Capture the situation, motivation, and expected outcome components
- Note the context and circumstances described by customers
- Document triggers, barriers, and outcomes mentioned
- Save examples of job mapping and timeline analysis
- Record any related jobs or sub-jobs identified

## Skill Overview

The jobs-to-be-done (JTBD) skill enables practitioners to understand the jobs customers are trying to get done when they seek solutions, focusing on the progress they want to make in specific circumstances. Unlike problem-focused or product-centric approaches, this approach centers on the customer's goal and the hiring decision they make to achieve progress, providing deeper insight into motivation, context, and innovation opportunities.

## Key Differentiators

- Focuses on the job (progress sought) rather than just the problem or product
- Uses a specific statement format: "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome]."
- Considers the context and circumstances that trigger the job search
- Analyzes the functional, social, and emotional dimensions of the job sought
- Examines triggers, barriers, and outcomes related to the job
- Applies JTBD insights to solution development, positioning, and messaging

## Common Applications

- Understanding why customers seek solutions for a problem
- Informing value propositions and positioning around customer progress
- Identifying innovation opportunities by examining where current solutions fall short
- Developing solutions that help customers get the whole job done better
- Communicating with customers in terms of their goals and context

## Success Indicators

After applying this skill, you should be able to:
- Conduct JTBD discovery using interviews, mapping, and timeline analysis
- Articulate clear job statements with situation, motivation, and expected outcome
- Analyze job structure, triggers, barriers, and desired outcomes
- Identify innovation opportunities where current solutions inadequately serve the job
- Develop solutions that help customers get the job done better
- Use JTBD language in value propositions, positioning, and messaging

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Focusing only on the problem without considering the progress the customer seeks
- Writing job statements that are vague, situational, or missing components
- Overlooking the context and circumstances that trigger the job search
- Ignoring the social and emotional dimensions of the job sought
- Failing to examine what triggers customers to seek solutions and what barriers they face
- Not applying JTBD insights to solution development and customer communication

## Next Steps

After completing JTBD analysis, consider:
- Developing solutions that address the whole job, not just isolated features
- Creating value propositions that frame the product in terms of the job it helps get done
- Building positioning statements that address the situation, motivation, and expected outcome
- Identifying and addressing barriers that prevent customers from getting the job done
- Sharing JTBD insights with the venture team and advisors
