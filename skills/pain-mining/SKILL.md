# Pain Mining Skill

This skill provides a structured approach to discovering, validating, and quantifying customer pains - the problems, frustrations, and unmet needs that create motivation for change and willingness to pay.

## When to Use This Skill

- Problem discovery and validation
- Understanding the severity and frequency of customer problems
- Testing problem hypotheses and assumptions
- Quantifying pain for value proposition and pricing decisions
- Identifying workarounds and compensatory behaviors
- Building empathy for customer experiences

## Core Principles

1. **Pain is motivational** - Without sufficient pain, there's no impetus for change
2. **Listen for pain language** - Frustration, annoyance, worry, stress, fear
3. **Seek specific incidents** - Not general opinions but concrete experiences
4. **Measure frequency and intensity** - How often and how bad is it?
5. **Distinguish symptoms from root causes** - Treat the disease, not just symptoms
6. **Validate workarounds** - What people do when they can't solve the problem
7. **Consider cost of inaction** - What happens if nothing changes?

## Pain Dimensions Framework

When evaluating pain, consider these dimensions:

### Frequency
- How often does this occur? (Daily, weekly, monthly, rarely)
- Is it predictable or random?
- Are there triggering events or conditions?

### Intensity
- How bad is it when it happens? (Mild annoyance to debilitating)
- Physical, emotional, financial, social, or time costs?
- What's the worst aspect of this pain?

### Duration
- How long does each occurrence last?
- Is it acute (short-term) or chronic (long-term)?
- Does it have lingering effects?

### Scope
- How many people are affected?
- Is it widespread or niche?
- Are there different segments with different pain profiles?

### Cost of Inaction
- What happens if this pain is not addressed?
- Financial losses, missed opportunities, health impacts, etc.
- What are people currently tolerating?

## Pain Discovery Process

### 1. Preparation
- Define the problem space or domain to explore
- Review existing evidence and assumptions
- Prepare open-ended questions focused on experiences
- Consider ethical considerations for sensitive topics

### 2. Discovery Techniques

#### a. Incident Recall
- "Tell me about the last time this happened..."
- "Walk me through a recent episode from start to finish..."
- "What was the most recent time you dealt with this?"

#### b. Pain Timeline
- "When did you first notice this problem?"
- "How has it changed over time?"
- "What made it better or worse at different points?"

#### c. Impact Exploration
- "How does this affect your work/day/life?"
- "What do you have to do differently because of this?"
- "What have you given up or avoided because of this?"

#### d. Workaround Identification
- "What have you tried to do about this?"
- "What solutions have you attempted?"
- "What shortcuts or temporary fixes do you use?"

#### e. Cost Inquiry
- "How much time does this cost you each week?"
- "What financial impact has this had?"
- "What opportunities have you missed because of this?"

### 3. Pain Validation

#### a. Severity Scaling
- On a scale of 1-10, how bad is this pain?
- What would make it a 10? What would make it a 1?
- How does this compare to other problems you have?

#### b. Frequency Confirmation
- How many times has this happened in the last week/month?
- Is this typical or atypical?
- What factors influence how often it occurs?

#### c. Alternative Testing
- If you could wave a wand and fix this, what would you do differently?
- What would you be able to do if this problem disappeared?
- How much would you pay to make this go away?

### 4. Pain Articulation

#### Problem Statement Format
[Who] experiences [problem] when [context/situation], leading to [impact/consequence].

#### Example:
"Small business owners experience costly late accessibility rework when launching new features, leading to delayed releases and budget overruns."

#### Pain Hierarchy
- Primary pains (directly experienced)
- Secondary pains (consequences or ripple effects)
- Related pains (in same problem domain)

## Evidence Collection

- Record verbatim descriptions of pain experiences
- Note specific incidents with dates, times, and context
- Capture emotional language and physical sensations
- Document workarounds and their effectiveness
- Measure frequency, duration, and intensity when possible
- Separate pain descriptions from interpretations or theories
- Label assumptions about pain causes or solutions

## Skill Overview

The pain mining skill enables practitioners to systematically uncover and analyze customer pains - the problems, frustrations, and unmet needs that drive human behavior and create market opportunities. Unlike general problem discovery, pain mining focuses specifically on the motivational aspects of problems: how bad they are, how often they occur, and what they cost people.

## Key Differentiators

- Focuses on pain severity and frequency, not just problem existence
- Uses specific incident recall rather than general opinions
- Measures pain across multiple dimensions
- Validates pain through workarounds and cost of inaction
- Distinguishes between symptoms and root causes

## Common Applications

- Validating problem hypotheses
- Quantifying market opportunity and willingness to pay
- Understanding customer decision-making triggers
- Identifying early adopters and high-pain segments
- Informing value proposition and messaging
- Prioritizing features based on pain relief
- Building empathy for customer experiences

## Success Indicators

After applying this skill, you should be able to:
- Conduct interviews that reveal specific pain incidents
- Collect detailed descriptions of pain experiences
- Measure pain frequency, intensity, and impact
- Identify patterns in when and how pain occurs
- Distinguish between primary pains and secondary effects
- Generate hypotheses about pain causes and solutions
- Avoid confusing symptoms with root causes

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Confusing pain with general dissatisfaction
- Asking about solutions before understanding pain
- Treating all mentions of a problem as equal pain
- Focusing on frequency without considering intensity
- Ignoring emotional and social dimensions of pain
- Assuming pain is universal without segmentation
- Forgetting to ask about workarounds and coping mechanisms
- Not validating pain severity with behavioral evidence

## Next Steps

After completing pain mining, consider:
- Creating assumption maps from pain insights
- Designing experiments to test pain hypotheses
- Developing value propositions based on pain relief
- Segmenting customers by pain profiles
- Prioritization exercises for feature development
- Sharing pain insights with the venture team
