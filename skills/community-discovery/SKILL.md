# Community Discovery Skill

This skill provides a structured approach to discovering, understanding, and engaging with communities that are relevant to a venture's problem space, solution, or target customers. Unlike broad market research, this skill focuses on the specific social structures, communication patterns, gathering places, and influence dynamics within communities that can affect venture success.

## When to Use This Skill

- After initial problem discovery and customer identification
- Before developing community-focused strategies or tactics
- When planning outreach, engagement, or advocacy efforts
- To understand where target customers gather and how they communicate
- To identify community leaders, influencers, and gatekeepers

## Core Principles

1. **Communities are not just demographics** - Focus on shared identity, interactions, and shared practices
2. **Observe before participating** - Understand community norms and culture before engaging
3. **Value exchange, not extraction** - Communities benefit from participation, not just ventures
4. **Multiple community types** - Consider geographic, interest-based, practice-based, and circumstance-based communities
5. **Language and culture matter** - Communities have their own languages, norms, and taboos

## Community Types Framework

### 1. Geographic Communities
- **Definition**: Communities based on shared location or territory
- **Examples**: Neighborhoods, cities, regions, countries
- **Relevance**: Local problems, location-specific regulations, area-specific needs
- **Discovery Methods**: Geographic data, local government sources, area-specific publications

### 2. Interest-Based Communities
- **Definition**: Communities formed around shared interests, hobbies, or passions
- **Examples**: Gaming communities, fitness groups, hobby clubs, fan communities
- **Relevance**: Passion-driven problems, enthusiast markets, lifestyle-oriented solutions
- **Discovery Methods**: Forum analysis, social media groups, event platforms, publication tracking

### 3. Practice-Based Communities (Communities of Practice)
- **Definition**: Communities of people who share a profession, craft, or practice
- **Examples**: Developer communities, designer networks, teacher associations, professional guilds
- **Relevance**: Professional problems, industry-specific challenges, skill-based solutions
- **Discovery Methods**: Professional associations, conference attendance, certification bodies, trade publications

### 4. Circumstance-Based Communities
- **Definition**: Communities formed by shared life situations or experiences
- **Examples**: Parent groups, disease support communities, immigrant communities, veteran networks
- **Relevance**: Life-stage problems, experience-specific challenges, situation-tailored solutions
- **Discovery Methods**: Support organizations, nonprofit networks, advocacy groups, healthcare providers

### 5. Virtual Communities
- **Definition**: Communities primarily existing in digital spaces
- **Examples**: Subreddits, Discord servers, Facebook groups, specialized forums
- **Relevance**: Online problems, digital-native audiences, remote collaboration needs
- **Discovery Methods**: Platform analysis, hashtag tracking, community discovery tools, network analysis

## Community Discovery Process

### 1. Preparation
- Clearly define the problem space and potential customer segments
- Identify hypotheses about where relevant communities might exist
- Prepare community discovery templates
- Consider ethical guidelines for community observation and engagement

### 2. Community Identification

#### Hypothesis-Driven Search
- Based on problem insights: "Where would people with this problem gather?"
- Based on solution ideas: "Where would people interested in this solution be?"
- Based on customer characteristics: "Where do these demographics/psychographics congregate?"

#### Discovery Channels
- **Online Platforms**: Reddit, Facebook, LinkedIn, Discord, Slack, forums
- **Offline Venues**: Meetups, conferences, trade shows, local gatherings, classes
- **Publications**: Newsletters, blogs, magazines, industry publications
- **Organizations**: Nonprofits, associations, clubs, advocacy groups
- **Events**: Workshops, webinars, meetups, conventions

#### Community Signals
- High engagement and activity levels
- Specific language and terminology
- Recurring themes and discussion topics
- Shared rituals, events, or traditions
- Clear membership criteria or participation barriers

### 3. Community Observation

#### Lurking and Listening
- Observe community interactions without participating initially
- Note the community's language, norms, and etiquette
- Identify hot topics, recurring debates, and community values
- Observe how newcomers are welcomed or questioned

#### Content Analysis
- Analyze popular posts, discussions, and shared content
- Identify frequently asked questions and common pain points
- Note the tone and emotional temperature of discussions
- Track changes in conversation topics over time

#### Member Identification
- Identify regular contributors and community leaders
- Note different roles (moderators, experts, helpers, newcomers)
- Observe influence patterns and authority structures
- Identify gatekeepers and boundary maintainers

### 4. Community Understanding

#### Community Mapping
- Map community structure: leadership, subgroups, roles, and relationships
- Identify community boundaries: who is in/out and why
- Map communication flows: how information spreads within the community
- Identify community resources: shared knowledge, tools, and references

#### Needs and Motivations Analysis
- Understand why members participate in the community
- Identify what members seek to gain (information, belonging, status, etc.)
- Note problems and frustrations members discuss about the community itself
- Understand what would make members leave or reduce participation

#### Cultural Analysis
- Document community language, terminology, and inside jokes
- Identify norms, taboos, and acceptable behaviors
- Observe rituals, traditions, and recurring events
- Note the community's history and origin story

### 5. Community Engagement Planning

#### Value Proposition for Community
- What unique value can we bring to the community?
- How can we help the community achieve its goals?
- What problems within the community can we help solve?
- How can we contribute without extracting or disrupting?

#### Engagement Strategy
- Initial observation and listening phase
- Value-first participation (answering questions, sharing resources)
- Gradual increase in participation based on trust built
- Clear identification of venture affiliation when appropriate

#### Ethical Considerations
- Respect community rules, norms, and expectations
- Avoid spamming, self-promotion, or disruptive behavior
- Be transparent about venture affiliation when relevant
- Consider reciprocity and mutual benefit in all interactions

## Evidence Collection

- Record community names, platforms, and access methods
- Document community size, activity levels, and growth trends
- Capture community language, terminology, and communication patterns
- Note community norms, rules, and etiquette
- Identify community leaders, influencers, and gatekeepers
- Document community needs, motivations, and pain points
- Record observations about community structure and culture
- Save examples of community interactions and content
- Note any changes in community dynamics over time

## Skill Overview

The community discovery skill enables practitioners to systematically discover, understand, and engage with communities relevant to their venture's problem space or target customers. Unlike treating audiences as mere demographics, this approach focuses on the social structures, communication patterns, gathering places, and influence dynamics within communities that can significantly affect venture success.

## Key Differentiators

- Focuses on communities as social systems with shared identity and practices
- Distinguishes between geographic, interest-based, practice-based, circumstance-based, and virtual communities
- Emphasizes observation and listening before community participation
- Provides frameworks for understanding community structure, norms, and culture
- Identifies community leaders, influencers, and gatekeepers
- Prioritizes value exchange and community benefit in engagement strategies
- Addresses ethical considerations in community discovery and engagement

## Common Applications

- Discovering where target customers gather online and offline
- Understanding community language and communication patterns
- Identifying community needs, frustrations, and aspirations
- Planning community engagement and outreach strategies
- Identifying potential partnerships with community leaders or organizations
- Understanding barriers to entry and acceptance within communities

## Success Indicators

After applying this skill, you should be able to:
- Identify relevant communities using multiple discovery channels
- Observe and document community norms, language, and communication patterns
- Map community structure including leadership, roles, and relationships
- Identify community leaders, influencers, and gatekeepers
- Understand community needs, motivations, and pain points
- Develop engagement strategies that prioritize community value and benefit
- Address ethical considerations in community discovery and engagement

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Treating communities as mere demographic segments
- Participating in communities before understanding their norms and culture
- Extracting value from communities without providing reciprocal benefit
- Using spamming or disruptive tactics to gain community attention
- Failing to identify community leaders, influencers, and gatekeepers
- Overlooking community-specific language, terminology, and communication patterns
- Ignoring community norms, rules, and etiquette in engagement efforts
- Not considering reciprocity and mutual benefit in community interactions

## Next Steps

After completing community discovery, consider:
- Developing community-specific value propositions and messaging
- Planning community engagement and outreach strategies
- Identifying potential community partnerships or collaborations
- Creating community-focused content and resources
- Building relationships with community leaders and influencers
- Monitoring community dynamics and changes over time
