# Competitor Mapping Skill

This skill provides a structured approach to analyzing competitors to inform venture strategy, positioning, and differentiation. Unlike casual competitor observation, this skill emphasizes systematic collection, organization, and analysis of competitor data to identify opportunities and threats.

## When to Use This Skill

- After problem discovery and before solution ideation
- When developing positioning and differentiation strategies
- Before pricing strategy development
- To identify market gaps and opportunities
- To understand competitive threats and challenges

## Core Principles

1. **Focus on customer-relevant competitors** - Analyze competitors that customers actually consider
2. **Look beyond features** - Analyze business models, strategies, and customer perceptions
3. **Customer perspective is key** - How do customers perceive and compare competitors?
4. **Identify gaps and weaknesses** - Find where competitors fail to meet customer needs
5. **Ethical and legal methods** - Use only publicly available and ethically gathered information

## Competitor Types Framework

### 1. Direct Competitors
- **Definition**: Businesses offering similar solutions to the same target customers
- **Examples**: Other accessibility auditing tools for web developers
- **Analysis Focus**: Feature comparison, pricing, positioning, and customer perception

### 2. Indirect Competitors
- **Definition**: Businesses offering different solutions that solve the same customer problem
- **Examples**: Manual accessibility consulting, browser extensions, plugins
- **Analysis Focus**: Problem-solving approaches, trade-offs, and customer choices

### 3. Replacement Competitors
- **Definition**: Businesses offering solutions that make the problem obsolete or unnecessary
- **Examples**: Automated accessibility fixes, AI-powered code generation
- **Analysis Focus**: Disruption potential, adoption barriers, and customer value shifts

### 4. Potential Competitors
- **Definition**: Businesses not currently in the market but could enter easily
- **Examples**: Large tech companies adding features, open-source projects
- **Analysis Focus**: Entry barriers, motivation signals, and market timing

## Competitor Mapping Process

### 1. Preparation
- Clearly define the problem space, target customer, and solution concept
- Identify hypotheses about who competitors might be
- Prepare competitor mapping templates
- Consider ethical guidelines for competitor research

### 2. Competitor Identification

#### Discovery Methods
- **Customer Interviews**: "What alternatives do you currently use or consider?"
- **Keyword Research**: Search for problem-solving terms and related solutions
- **App Store and Marketplace Search**: Look for similar products in relevant categories
- **Industry Reports and Analyses**: Use market research, analyst reports, and industry publications
- **Social Listening**: Monitor discussions about problem solutions
- **Events and Conferences**: See who is presenting or exhibiting in the space
- **Investment and Funding Data**: Look at venture capital investments in the area

#### Competitor Signals
- Appears in customer consideration sets
- Solves the same or similar problem
- Targets similar customer segments
- Has visible market presence or activity

### 3. Competitor Categorization

#### By Competitor Type
- Direct, indirect, replacement, potential
- Primary, secondary, tertiary competitors

#### By Business Model
- Subscription, freemium, transactional, advertising, etc.
- Open source, proprietary, hybrid models

#### By Market Position
- Market leader, challenger, follower, niche player
- Innovator, imitator, consolidator

### 4. Competitor Analysis Framework

#### 1. Basic Information
- Name, website, founding date, headquarters
- Funding status, investor information
- Team size, key personnel
- Mission, vision, and value proposition (as stated)

#### 2. Product/Service Offering
- Core product/service description
- Features and capabilities
- Pricing structure and price points
- Target customer and market segments
- Distribution and delivery channels

#### 3. Market Position and Strategy
- Market share and growth trajectory
- Positioning statement and target audience
- Key differentiators and competitive advantages
- Marketing and acquisition strategies
- Partnerships and alliances

#### 4. Customer Perception and Reception
- Customer reviews and ratings
- Common praises and complaints
- Brand perception and reputation
- Customer loyalty and retention signals

#### 5. Financial and Performance Indicators
- Revenue estimates and growth rates (if available)
- Customer base size and growth
- Engagement and usage metrics
- Financial stability and investment history

#### 6. Strengths and Weaknesses
- What they do well (strengths)
- Where they fall short (weaknesses)
- Customer-perceived strengths and weaknesses
- Operational and strategic limitations

### 4. Competitor Analysis Process

#### Data Collection
- Website and product analysis
- Pricing page and feature analysis
- Customer review mining (app stores, review sites, forums)
- Social media analysis (engagement, sentiment, content)
- News and press release analysis
- Job posting analysis (hiring priorities, tech stack)
- Technology analysis (built-with, tech stack, integrations)
- Legal and regulatory filings (if applicable)

#### Analysis Techniques
- Feature comparison matrices
- Positioning mapping (perceptual maps)
- SWOT analysis (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats)
- Porter’s Five Forces analysis
- Business model canvas comparison
- Customer journey comparison
- Pricing strategy analysis

#### Evidence Collection
- Record all data with sources and dates
- Capture direct quotes and observations
- Note any assumptions or inferences made
- Label information as factual, observed, or inferred

## Skill Overview

The competitor mapping skill enables practitioners to systematically analyze competitors to inform venture strategy, positioning, and differentiation. Unlike casual competitor observation, this approach emphasizes structured data collection, organization, and analysis to understand the competitive landscape from the customer's perspective, identify opportunities, and anticipate threats.

## Key Differentiators

- Focuses on competitors that customers actually consider and compare
- Analyzes beyond features to include business models, strategies, and customer perceptions
- Emphasizes the customer perspective in competitor analysis
- Provides frameworks for identifying competitor strengths, weaknesses, and gaps
- Uses structured templates for consistent competitor profiling and comparison
- Addresses ethical considerations in competitor research

## Common Applications

- Understanding the competitive landscape before solution ideation
- Informing positioning and differentiation strategies
- Identifying market gaps and opportunities for innovation
- Developing pricing strategies based on competitive positioning
- Assessing competitive threats and challenges
- Building investor-ready competitive analysis

## Success Indicators

After applying this skill, you should be able to:
- Identify relevant competitors using multiple discovery channels
- Categorize competitors by type, business model, and market position
- Collect and organize data on competitors' products, pricing, positioning, and performance
- Analyze competitor strengths and weaknesses with supporting evidence
- Understand how customers perceive and compare competitors
- Identify market gaps and opportunities based on competitor analysis
- Create structured competitor profiles and comparisons

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Focusing only on direct competitors while ignoring indirect and replacement threats
- Analyzing only features without considering business models and customer perceptions
- Relying on outdated or incomplete competitor information
- Putting the venture at the center of analysis rather than the customer perspective
- Using unethical or illegal methods to gather competitor information
- Creating competitor analyses that are not actionable for venture strategy

## Next Steps

After completing competitor mapping, consider:
- Developing positioning strategies based on competitor gaps and customer perceptions
- Informing pricing strategy through competitive pricing analysis
- Identifying partnership or acquisition opportunities
- Building competitive battle cards for sales and marketing
- Sharing competitor analysis with the venture team and advisors
