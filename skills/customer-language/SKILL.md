# Customer Language Skill

This skill provides a structured approach to capturing, organizing, and leveraging the exact words, phrases, and expressions customers use when describing their problems, needs, desires, and experiences. Unlike paraphrasing or summarizing customer input, this skill emphasizes preserving the authentic voice of the customer to ensure resonance in messaging, positioning, and solution development.

## When to Use This Skill

- During and after customer interviews and conversations
- When analyzing survey responses, reviews, or feedback
- Before crafting value propositions, positioning statements, or marketing messages
- To ensure solution messaging resonates with target customers
- When creating scripts for outbound outreach or sales conversations

## Core Principles

1. **Authenticity over paraphrasing** - Use customers' exact words when possible
2. **Context matters** - Capture not just what was said, but how and why it was said
3. **Patterns over anecdotes** - Look for recurring language across multiple customers
4. **Language informs positioning** - Customer language should shape how we talk about the problem and solution
5. **Respect the customer voice** - Avoid putting words in customers' mouths or "correcting" their language

## Customer Language Collection

### 1. Direct Capture Methods

#### Customer Interviews
- Record verbatim quotes with speaker identification
- Note tone, emphasis, and emotional context
- Use quotation marks for exact phrases
- Avoid cleaning up grammar or filler words in verbatim transcripts

#### Surveys and Feedback
- Export open-ended responses exactly as submitted
- Preserve original spelling, punctuation, and capitalization
- Note any emojis, slang, or informal language
- Keep responses separate for individual analysis

#### Reviews and Social Media
- Screenshot or copy-paste exact text from public sources
- Preserve original formatting and platform-specific elements
- Note the context (product review, comment thread, etc.)
- Source and date each piece of language

#### Observational Notes
- Write down exact phrases heard in context
- Note non-verbal cues that accompanied the language
- Use shorthand or symbols only if you can reliably expand them later

### 2. Indirect Capture Methods

#### Support Tickets and Logs
- Extract customer descriptions of problems and requests
- Preserve original language in ticket notes and chat logs
- Note frequency and patterns across similar tickets

#### Sales Call Recordings
- Transcribe or note exact customer phrases and questions
- Capture objections, hesitations, and enthusiasms in customer's words
- Document language used when describing current solutions

#### User Testing Sessions
- Record think-aloud protocol verbatim
- Capture frustration expressions and delight moments in customer's language
- Note language used when describing expectations vs. reality

## Customer Language Organization

### 1. Language Banking

#### Problem Language
- Words and phrases customers use to describe the problem
- Symptoms, pains, frustrations, and negative experiences
- Impact descriptions and consequence language

#### Desire Language
- Words and phrases customers use to express wants and needs
- Ideal state descriptions and aspiration language
- Benefit language and value expressions

#### Solution Language
- Words and phrases customers use to describe current solutions
- Likes, dislikes, and workarounds they mention
- Language used when comparing alternatives

#### Emotional Language
- Words expressing emotions related to the problem or solution
- Intensity modifiers and emotional qualifiers
- Metaphors and analogies customers use

### 2. Categorization Framework

#### By Source
- Interviews: [Customer ID or pseudonym]
- Surveys: [Response ID or anonymous]
- Reviews: [Platform, username, date]
- Support: [Ticket ID, date]
- Social: [Platform, handle, date]

#### By Theme
- Problem severity: "This is killing our business," "We lose hours every week"
- Frequency: "This happens every single time," "We deal with this daily"
- Impact: "It costs us thousands," "It's damaging our reputation"
- Emotion: "We're frustrated," "This is incredibly stressful"
- Desire: "We wish we could," "It would be amazing if"
- Solution language: "We currently," "We tried X but Y happened"

#### By Customer Segment
- Language patterns by demographics, firmographics, or behavior
- Segment-specific terminology and jargon
- Differences in how segments describe the same concept

## Customer Language Analysis

### 1. Frequency Analysis

#### Word and Phrase Frequency
- Count occurrences of specific words and phrases
- Identify high-frequency language across multiple customers
- Note variations and synonyms for the same concept

#### Theme Frequency
- Count mentions of specific problem themes
- Track frequency of emotional expressions
- Measure consistency of language across segments

### 2. Pattern Identification

#### Recurring Expressions
- Identify exact phrases used by multiple customers
- Note language that appears in unrelated conversations
- Look for metaphors and analogies that appear repeatedly

#### Language Shifts
- Track how language changes across the customer journey
- Note differences between problem description and solution description
- Observe how language evolves after solution experience

#### Emotional Resonance
- Measure emotional intensity of language
- Identify language that triggers strong positive or negative reactions
- Note language that creates sense of urgency or importance

### 3. Language Quality Assessment

#### Specificity
- Is the language specific and concrete or vague and abstract?
- Does it include measurable quantities or clear timeframes?
- Does it avoid jargon and technical terms when speaking to non-experts?

#### Authenticity
- Does the language sound natural for the customer segment?
- Does it match how the customer speaks in other contexts?
- Is it free from interviewer or researcher influence?

#### Actionability
- Can the language be used directly in messaging and positioning?
- Does it convey clear value or problem statements?
- Does it avoid passive voice or weak expressions?

## Customer Language Application

### 1. Messaging and Positioning

#### Value Proposition Development
- Use customer problem language to articulate the problem being solved
- Incorporate customer desire language to express the wanted outcome
- Use customer benefit language to describe solution advantages

#### Positioning Statements
- Frame the problem using customer language
- Describe the solution using customer benefit expressions
- Differentiate using language customers use when comparing alternatives

#### Marketing Copy
- Use verbatim customer quotes in testimonials and case studies
- Incorporate customer language in headlines and subheadings
- Use customer language in email subject lines and ad copy

### 2. Solution Development

#### Feature Naming
- Use customer language to name features and capabilities
- Avoid internal jargon in customer-facing terminology
- Name features after the customer benefit they provide

#### User Interface Copy
- Use customer language for button labels, menu items, and instructions
- Incorporate customer language in error messages and help text
- Use customer language in onboarding and tutorial content

#### Sales and Outbound
- Use customer language in cold email and LinkedIn outreach
- Incorporate customer language in discovery call questions
- Use customer language to frame demos and presentations

### 3. Internal Alignment

#### Team Communication
- Share customer language bank with product, marketing, and sales teams
- Use customer language in internal documents and presentations
- Reference customer language when making product decisions

#### Investor Communications
- Use customer language to describe the problem and opportunity
- Incorporate customer quotes in pitch decks and investor updates
- Use customer language to explain traction and validation

## Skill Overview

The customer language skill enables practitioners to capture, organize, and leverage the exact words and phrases customers use when describing their problems, needs, and experiences. Unlike paraphrasing or summarizing customer input, this approach preserves the authentic voice of the customer to ensure that messaging, positioning, and solution development resonate with the target audience.

## Key Differentiators

- Focuses on capturing verbatim customer language rather than paraphrased summaries
- Preserves original formatting, spelling, and punctuation for authenticity
- Organizes language by type (problem, desire, solution, emotional) and source
- Applies frequency analysis and pattern identification to customer language
- Directly applies customer language to messaging, positioning, and solution development
- Respects the customer voice and avoids putting words in customers' mouths

## Common Applications

- Capturing language from customer interviews and discovery calls
- Analyzing open-ended survey responses and feedback
- Mining customer reviews, testimonials, and social media
- Organizing language from support tickets and user testing sessions
- Developing value propositions and positioning statements
- Creating marketing copy, sales scripts, and user interface text

## Success Indicators

After applying this skill, you should be able to:
- Collect verbatim customer language from multiple sources
- Organize language by type, source, and thematic categories
- Perform frequency analysis to identify high-frequency words and phrases
- Identify recurring expressions and language patterns across customers
- Assess language quality for specificity, authenticity, and actionability
- Apply customer language to value propositions, positioning statements, and messaging
- Use customer language in feature naming, user interface copy, and sales conversations

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Paraphrasing customer language instead of capturing verbatim quotes
- Losing context by not recording source, date, and circumstances
- Failing to categorize language by type and missing patterns
- Using customer language without assessing its quality and authenticity
- Applying language without considering the customer segment or context
- Putting words in customers' mouths or "correcting" their language
- Using internal jargon instead of customer language in customer-facing materials

## Next Steps

After completing customer language collection and analysis, consider:
- Developing value propositions using customer problem and benefit language
- Crafting positioning statements that use customer language for differentiation
- Creating marketing copy that incorporates verbatim customer quotes
- Naming features and capabilities using customer benefit language
- Writing user interface instructions and help text in customer language
- Developing sales call scripts that use customer discovery language
