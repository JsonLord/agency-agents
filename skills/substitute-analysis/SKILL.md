# Substitute Analysis Skill

This skill provides a structured approach to identifying and analyzing substitute solutions that customers might use to solve their problem instead of the venture's proposed solution. Unlike competitor analysis which focuses on similar solutions, substitute analysis looks at different approaches that address the same underlying need or problem.

## When to Use This Skill

- After problem discovery and validation
- When developing positioning and differentiation strategies
- To understand the full range of solutions customers consider
- Before pricing strategy development
- To identify potential threats from unexpected quarters

## Core Principles

1. **Substitutes solve the same problem differently** - Focus on solutions that address the core customer need through different means
2. **Look beyond obvious competitors** - Consider workarounds, manual processes, and doing nothing
3. **Customer perspective is essential** - What do customers actually consider as alternatives?
4. **Substitutes can be non-consumption** - The biggest substitute is often doing nothing or living with the problem
5. **Analyze switching barriers and triggers** - Understand what would cause customers to switch from substitutes to your solution

## Substitute Types Framework

### 1. Direct Substitutes
- **Definition**: Different solutions that solve the same problem in a different way
- **Examples**: For accessibility testing: manual audits vs automated tools, browser plugins vs standalone software
- **Analysis Focus**: Trade-offs, switching costs, and customer preferences

### 2. Indirect Substitutes
- **Definition**: Solutions that address a related problem or partially solve the main problem
- **Examples**: For accessibility testing: general QA testing, usability testing, SEO audits
- **Analysis Focus**: Scope, completeness, and value proposition gaps

### 3. Behavioral Substitutes
- **Definition**: Customer behaviors or workarounds that avoid or mitigate the problem
- **Examples**: Ignoring accessibility issues, fixing only after complaints, outsourcing to specialists
- **Analysis Focus**: Workaround effectiveness, satisfaction levels, and pain points

### 4. Non-Consumption
- **Definition**: Choosing not to solve the problem at all
- **Examples**: Living with inaccessible websites, accepting legal risk, avoiding web development
- **Analysis Focus**: Problem tolerance, risk perception, and inertia

## Substitute Analysis Process

### 1. Preparation
- Clearly define the customer problem and desired outcome
- Identify hypotheses about what substitutes customers might use
- Prepare substitute analysis templates
- Consider ethical guidelines for gathering substitute information

### 2. Substitute Identification

#### Discovery Methods
- **Customer Interviews**: "What do you currently do about [problem]?", "What have you tried in the past?", "What would you do if you couldn't use our solution?"
- **Problem Ethnography**: Observe how customers currently handle the problem
- **Workaround Mining**: Search for customer discussions about workarounds and hacks
- **Support Forum Analysis**: Look at questions and solutions in technical forums
- **Review Mining**: Analyze customer reviews for mentions of alternatives
- **Job Posting Analysis**: See what skills companies are hiring for related to the problem

#### Substitute Signals
- Customers mention it when discussing the problem
- Observed in customer behavior or workflows
- Appears in search results for problem-solving
- Referenced in customer reviews or feedback

### 3. Substitute Categorization

#### By Substitute Type
- Direct, indirect, behavioral, non-consumption

#### By Availability
- Readily available, requires effort, requires expertise, not available

#### By Cost to Customer
- Free, low cost, moderate cost, high cost

#### By Switching Barrier
- Low (easy to switch), medium, high (difficult to switch)

### 4. Substitute Analysis Framework

#### 1. Substitute Description
- What the substitute is and how it works
- Who provides or creates it
- How customers access or use it

#### 2. Problem-Solution Fit
- How well does it address the customer problem?
- What aspects of the problem does it solve vs. leave unresolved?
- What is the quality or effectiveness of the solution?

#### 3. Customer Experience
- What is it like to use this substitute?
- What do customers like about it?
- What do customers dislike or find frustrating?

#### 4. Costs and Investment
- What financial cost does it involve?
- What time, effort, or learning is required?
- What social or psychological costs are involved?

#### 5. Switching Barriers
- What would make it difficult for customers to switch away from this substitute?
- What would make it easy for customers to switch to a better solution?

#### 6. Triggers for Change
- What events or circumstances cause customers to reconsider their substitute?
- What problems with the substitute lead to active searching for alternatives?

## Evidence Collection

- Record substitute descriptions with sources and dates
- Capture customer quotes about substitutes and workarounds
- Document problem-solution fit assessments
- Note customer experiences, likes, and dislikes
- Record costs, investments, and switching barriers
- Identify triggers for change and reconsideration
- Save examples of substitute usage and customer workflows

## Skill Overview

The substitute analysis skill enables practitioners to identify and analyze substitute solutions that customers might use to solve their problem instead of the venture's proposed solution. Unlike competitor analysis which focuses on similar solutions, this approach looks at different approaches, workarounds, and even non-consumption to understand the full landscape of how customers address their problem.

## Key Differentiators

- Focuses on solutions that solve the same problem through different means
- Looks beyond obvious competitors to include workarounds, manual processes, and doing nothing
- Emphasizes the customer perspective in understanding what substitutes they actually use
- Analyzes switching barriers that keep customers with their current substitutes
- Identifies triggers that cause customers to reconsider their current approach
- Considers non-consumption as a significant substitute option

## Common Applications

- Understanding the full range of solutions customers consider for a problem
- Informing positioning and differentiation strategies by highlighting unique value
- Identifying pricing strategy considerations based on substitute costs
- Building investor-ready analysis of the solution landscape
- Identifying potential threats from unexpected quarters
- Developing go-to-market strategies that address switching barriers

## Success Indicators

After applying this skill, you should be able to:
- Identify relevant substitutes using multiple discovery channels
- Categorize substitutes by type, availability, cost, and switching barrier
- Collect and organize data on substitutes' descriptions, problem-solution fit, and customer experience
- Analyze substitute strengths and weaknesses with supporting evidence
- Understand customer experiences, likes, and dislikes with current substitutes
- Identify switching barriers that maintain the status quo
- Recognize triggers that cause customers to seek better solutions
- Analyze non-consumption as a substitute option

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Focusing only on direct competitors while ignoring indirect substitutes and workarounds
- Overlooking behavioral substitutes and customer workarounds
- Forgetting that non-consumption (doing nothing) is often the primary substitute
- Analyzing substitutes without understanding the customer experience
- Failing to identify what makes it difficult or easy for customers to switch
- Not considering what events or problems cause customers to seek alternatives
- Creating substitute analyses that are not actionable for venture strategy

## Next Steps

After completing substitute analysis, consider:
- Developing positioning strategies that highlight advantages over substitutes
- Informing pricing strategy through substitute cost analysis
- Building battle cards that compare your solution to key substitutes
- Identifying customer segments most likely to switch based on substitute dissatisfaction
- Sharing substitute analysis with the venture team and advisors
