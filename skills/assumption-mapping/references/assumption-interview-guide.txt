# Assumption-Focused Interview Guide

## Learning Objectives
- [ ] Discover explicit and implicit assumptions
- [ ] Understand the reasoning behind assumptions
- [ ] Assess confidence levels in assumptions
- [ ] Identify assumptions by domain (desirability, viability, feasibility)

## Interview Structure

1. Opening (2-5 min)
   - Build rapport
   - State purpose: "Understanding what needs to be true for this idea to work"
   - Set expectations
   - Get consent

2. Warm-up (5-10 min)
   - Role/context questions
   - General experience questions

3. Assumption Discovery (20-30 min)
   - Start with the hypothesis: "We believe that [hypothesis]"
   - Ask: "What must be true for this to work?"
   - Use "5 Whys" to drill down
   - Explore each domain: desirability, viability, feasibility
   - Look for hidden assumptions

4. Closing (3-5 min)
   - Summary and thanks
   - Next steps
   - Any additional thoughts?

## Core Assumption Questions

### Desirability Assumptions
- "Who exactly has this problem?"
- "How do we know they find it painful enough to act?"
- "What evidence do we have that they want a solution?"
- "Why would they choose our solution over alternatives?"
- "What would make them not adopt this solution?"

### Viability Assumptions
- "How will we make money from this?"
- "What are the key costs we need to cover?"
- "Why would customers pay our price?"
- "How will we reach and acquire customers profitably?"
- "What could make this business model unsustainable?"

### Feasibility Assumptions
- "Can we actually build this with our resources?"
- "What technical challenges might we face?"
- "Do we have the necessary skills and expertise?"
- "How long will development really take?"
- "What dependencies do we have that could delay us?"

## Probing for Assumption Details

### Entity Clarification
- "Who specifically are we talking about?"
- "Can you give me an example of this person/entity?"
- "Are there different types of [entity] we should consider?"

### Action Specificity
- "What exactly will they do or experience?"
- "How will we know when this happens?"
- "What would count as evidence this occurred?"

### Basis Exploration
- "What makes you believe this will happen?"
- "What data or experience supports this belief?"
- "Is this based on analogy, data, opinion, or theory?"

### Confidence Assessment
- "On a scale of 1-10, how confident are you in this assumption?"
- "What would make you more or less confident?"
- "What evidence would change your mind?"

## Things to Avoid

- Leading questions that suggest specific assumptions
- Accepting vague assumptions without making them specific
- Confusing assumptions with facts or data
- Forgetting to ask about the reasoning behind assumptions
- Not distinguishing between explicit and implicit assumptions
