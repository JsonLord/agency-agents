# Customer Interview Skill

This skill provides a standardized approach to conducting customer interviews that uncover genuine pains, needs, and behaviors while avoiding bias and leading questions.

## When to Use This Skill

- Problem discovery and validation
- Understanding customer behaviors and motivations
- Testing value propositions and messaging
- Gathering feedback on prototypes or concepts
- Validating assumptions about customer needs

## Core Principles

1. **Start with curiosity, not assumptions**
2. **Listen more than you speak**
3. **Seek specific stories, not opinions**
4. **Measure with behavior, not intent**
5. **Embrace silence and discomfort**

## Interview Process

### 1. Preparation
- Define clear learning objectives
- Develop an interview guide with open-ended questions
- Identify and recruit target customers
- Prepare recording and note-taking tools
- Review ethical considerations and consent requirements

### 2. Opening (2-5 minutes)
- Build rapport and express gratitude
- Explain the purpose (without biasing)
- Set expectations for time and format
- Obtain consent for recording/notes
- Remind them there are no right/wrong answers

### 3. Warm-up Questions (5-10 minutes)
- Ask about their role/context
- Inquire about recent relevant experiences
- Establish comfort before diving deep

### 4. Deep Dive (20-30 minutes)
- Follow the interview guide but remain flexible
- Use the "5 Whys" technique to get to root causes
- Ask for specific recent examples
- Listen for pain points, workarounds, and unmet needs
- Notice emotional cues and energy levels

### 5. Closing (3-5 minutes)
- Thank them for their time
- Ask if they have anything to add
- Explain next steps and how they'll be informed
- Provide any promised incentives

## Question Framework

### Open-Ended Starters
- "Tell me about the last time you..."
- "Walk me through how you..."
- "What was that like for you?"
- "Help me understand..."

### Probing Questions
- "What made it difficult?"
- "How did you deal with that?"
- "What would have made it better?"
- "Can you give me a specific example?"
- "What were you hoping would happen?"

### Avoid
- Leading questions ("Don't you hate when...?")
- Solution questions ("Would you pay for X?")
- Hypotheticals ("Would you use this if...?")
- Feature-focused questions
- Questions that can be answered with yes/no

## Evidence Collection

- Record verbatim quotes whenever possible
- Note behaviors and workarounds mentioned
- Capture emotional language and intensity
- Document context and constraints
- Separate facts from interpretations
- Label assumptions that emerge

## Skill Overview

The customer interview skill enables practitioners to gather high-quality evidence about customer problems, needs, and behaviors through structured conversations. Unlike surveys or focus groups, one-on-one interviews allow for deep exploration of individual experiences and the flexibility to follow interesting leads.

## Key Differentiators

- Focuses on past behavior rather than future intent
- Uses open-ended questioning to avoid bias
- Seeks specific examples rather than opinions
- Measures through stories and workarounds
- Emphasizes listening over talking

## Common Applications

- Validating problem hypotheses
- Understanding customer journeys
- Testing messaging and positioning
- Discovering unmet needs and pain points
- Building empathy for target customers
- Informing value proposition design

## Success Indicators

After applying this skill, you should be able to:
- Conduct interviews that make customers feel heard
- Collect specific examples of behaviors and pain points
- Identify patterns across multiple interviews
- Distinguish between symptoms and root causes
- Generate hypotheses for further testing
- Avoid common interviewing pitfalls

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Interviewing only friends or colleagues
- Asking about purchase intent too early
- Presenting solutions before understanding problems
- Focusing on demographics over psychographics
- Treating interviews as validation rather than discovery
- Forgetting to thank participants for their time

## Next Steps

After completing customer interviews, consider:
- Thematic analysis of collected evidence
- Creating assumption maps from insights
- Designing experiments to test key hypotheses
- Developing value propositions based on customer language
- Sharing findings with the venture team
