# Distribution Analysis Skill

This skill provides a structured approach to analyzing and evaluating different channels for reaching, acquiring, and delivering value to customers. Unlike guessing or imitating competitors, this skill emphasizes systematic evaluation of channel effectiveness, cost, scalability, and fit with the venture's value proposition and target customers.

## When to Use This Skill

- After value proposition and positioning validation
- When developing go-to-market and customer acquisition strategies
- To identify the most effective and efficient channels for reaching target customers
- Before significant investment in marketing and sales
- To optimize existing channel performance

## Core Principles

1. **Channels are not one-size-fits-all** - Evaluate channels based on specific venture and customer context
2. **Focus on customer acquisition cost (CAC) and effectiveness** - Measure what it costs to acquire a customer through each channel and how well it works
3. **Consider channel scalability and sustainability** - Assess whether a channel can grow with the venture and maintain performance
4. **Align with customer behavior** - Choose channels where target customers actually are and how they make decisions
5. **Test and iterate** - Channel effectiveness changes over time; validate with real experiments

## Channel Types Framework

### 1. Organic Channels
- **Definition**: Channels that don't require direct payment for placement
- **Examples**: Content marketing, SEO, social media (organic), email lists, referrals, word-of-mouth
- **Characteristics**: Lower direct cost, higher time/effort investment, slower to scale

### 2. Paid Channels
- **Definition**: Channels that require payment for placement or exposure
- **Examples**: Pay-per-click advertising, social media ads, sponsorships, influencer marketing, traditional advertising
- **Characteristics**: Direct cost per exposure/click, faster to scale, requires ongoing investment

### 3. Partnership Channels
- **Definition**: Channels that involve collaboration with other organizations or individuals
- **Examples**: Affiliate marketing, reseller partnerships, integrations, co-marketing, channel sales
- **Characteristics**: Shared costs and risks, dependent on partner performance, can provide access to new audiences

### 4. Sales Channels
- **Definition**: Channels involving direct selling efforts
- **Examples**: Direct sales force, inside sales, channel sales, consultative selling
- **Characteristics**: Higher cost per acquisition, complex sales cycles, relationship-driven

### 5. Product-Led Channels
- **Definition**: Channels where the product itself drives acquisition and growth
- **Examples**: Viral loops, referral programs, freemium models, embedded sharing, user-generated content
- **Characteristics**: Lower CAC at scale, dependent on product design, network effects possible

## Distribution Analysis Process

### 1. Preparation
- Clearly define the target customer and their decision-making process
- Identify hypotheses about effective channels
- Prepare distribution analysis templates

### 2. Channel Identification

#### Discovery Methods
- **Customer Interviews**: "Where do you go for information about [problem]?", "How did you learn about solutions you currently use?", "What influences your purchasing decisions?"
- **Competitor Analysis**: Where do competitors appear and how do they acquire customers?
- **Market Research**: Industry reports on channel effectiveness and usage
- **Analogous Businesses**: How do similar ventures acquire customers?
- **Channel Research Tools**: Platforms like SimilarWeb, Alexa, etc.

#### Channel Signals
- Customers mention the channel when discussing how they find solutions
- Competitors are actively using the channel
- The channel reaches the target demographic effectively
- The channel aligns with the venture's resources and capabilities

### 3. Channel Evaluation Framework

#### 1. Channel Description
- What the channel is and how it works
- Who provides or operates the channel
- How to access or use the channel

#### 2. Customer Reach and Targeting
- Can the channel reach the target customer demographic?
- What targeting options are available (demographics, interests, behaviors, etc.)?
- How precise is the targeting?

#### 3. Cost Structure
- What are the costs associated with using the channel?
- Is cost based on CPM, CPC, CPA, flat rate, revenue share, etc.?
- What are the typical or test costs?
- Are there setup, minimum spend, or ongoing costs?

#### 4. Effectiveness and Performance
- What are the typical conversion rates or response rates?
- What is the quality of leads or customers acquired?
- How measurable are the results?
- What is the time lag between exposure and conversion?

#### 5. Scalability and Sustainability
- Can the channel scale with increasing investment?
- Does performance degrade with scale?
- Are there limits to available inventory or audience?
- How sustainable are the results over time?

#### 6. Effort and Resources Required
- What time, skills, and resources are needed to use the channel effectively?
- Is expertise required or available?
- What ongoing management and optimization is needed?

#### 7. Fit with Venture and Value Proposition
- Does the channel align with the venture's brand and positioning?
- Can the venture's message be effectively communicated through the channel?
- Are there any restrictions or limitations on messaging?

### 4. Channel Testing and Validation

#### Experiments
- Small-scale tests to measure actual performance
- A/B testing of different channels or channel variations
- Pilot campaigns with clear success criteria

#### Measurement
- Track costs, exposures, leads, conversions, and customers
- Calculate cost per lead (CPL), cost per acquisition (CPA), and return on ad spend (ROAS)
- Assess lead quality and conversion to paying customers

#### Learning
- What worked and what didn't?
- What would improve performance?
- How do results compare to hypotheses and alternatives?

## Evidence Collection

- Record channel descriptions with sources and dates
- Capture customer quotes about how they find solutions
- Document competitor channel usage and effectiveness
- Record channel cost structures and pricing models
- Note targeting capabilities and reach
- Save test results, performance metrics, and learning

## Skill Overview

The distribution analysis skill enables practitioners to analyze and evaluate different channels for reaching, acquiring, and delivering value to customers. Unlike guessing or imitating competitors, this approach emphasizes systematic evaluation of channel effectiveness, cost, scalability, and fit with the venture's value proposition and target customers, ensuring that customer acquisition efforts are focused on the most effective and efficient channels.

## Key Differentiators

- Focuses on evaluating channels based on specific venture and customer context
- Emphasizes measuring customer acquisition cost (CAC) and effectiveness for each channel
- Considers channel scalability, sustainability, and required effort and resources
- Evaluates channel fit with the venture's value proposition and positioning
- Mandates testing channels with real experiments to validate hypotheses

## Common Applications

- Evaluating marketing channels for customer acquisition
- Assessing sales channels for complex or high-value offerings
- Identifying partnership opportunities for market access
- Developing product-led growth strategies
- Informing go-to-market and customer acquisition strategies

## Success Indicators

After applying this skill, you should be able to:
- Identify relevant channels using multiple discovery channels
- Evaluate channels using the framework (description, reach, cost, effectiveness, scalability, effort, fit)
- Conduct small-scale tests to measure actual channel performance
- Calculate customer acquisition cost (CAC) and other key metrics
- Assess channel fit with the venture's value proposition and target customers
- Develop a channel strategy based on evidence and testing

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Choosing channels based on what competitors are doing without validation
- Focusing only on cost or only on reach without considering both
- Overlooking the effort and resources required to use a channel effectively
- Failing to test channels before significant investment
- Ignoring channel fit with the venture's brand and positioning
- Not considering scalability and sustainability of channel performance

## Next Steps

After completing distribution analysis, consider:
- Developing a channel mix strategy based on evaluation and testing
- Building financial models based on channel CAC and conversion rates
- Creating channel-specific marketing and sales plans
- Sharing distribution analysis with the venture team and advisors
