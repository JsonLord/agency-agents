# Market Sizing Skill

This skill provides a structured approach to estimating the size of a venture's target market using multiple methodologies. Unlike relying on a single estimate or unfounded assumptions, this skill emphasizes triangulating market size estimates from different approaches to arrive at a more reliable range.

## When to Use This Skill

- After problem validation and before significant investment
- When developing financial projections and business models
- To assess the attractiveness and potential of a market
- Before seeking external investment
- To inform pricing, positioning, and go-to-market strategies

## Core Principles

1. **Triangulate multiple approaches** - Use top-down, bottom-up, and value-theory methods
2. **Ground in credible data** - Use reliable sources and verify assumptions
3. **Be specific about the target market** - Define who, what, where, and when precisely
4. **Show your work** - Document sources, assumptions, and calculations
5. **Update with new information** - Revise estimates as better data becomes available

## Market Definition

Before sizing the market, clearly define:

### 1. Who is the target customer?
- Specific customer segments
- Demographics, firmographics, psychographics
- Inclusion and exclusion criteria

### 2. What are you selling?
- Specific product or service offering
- Core problem being solved
- Key features and benefits

### 3. Where do they compete?
- Geographic scope (local, regional, national, global)
- Relevant jurisdictions or markets

### 4. When do they buy?
- Purchase frequency and timing
- Seasonal or cyclical patterns
- Trigger events or circumstances

## Market Sizing Approaches

### 1. Top-Down Approach

Start with a large, known market and narrow down to your target segment.

#### Steps
- Identify a broad market with reliable data
- Apply segmentation filters to narrow to your target
- Document each filter and its source
- Calculate the resulting market size

#### Data Sources
- Industry reports and analyses
- Government statistics and census data
- Trade association publications
- Analyst reports and market research
- Academic studies and publications

#### Common Filters
- Geographic filters
- Demographic filters (age, income, etc.)
- Firmographic filters (company size, industry, etc.)
- Behavioral filters (usage, adoption, etc.)
- Need-based filters (problem prevalence, etc.)

### 2. Bottom-Up Approach

Start with observable data and build up to the total market.

#### Steps
- Identify observable units (customers, transactions, etc.)
- Determine value per unit (price, willingness to pay, etc.)
- Estimate number of units in the target market
- Calculate total market size

#### Data Sources
- Customer interviews and surveys
- Sales data from analogous products
- Observable behavior (searches, social media, etc.)
- Pilot tests and early customer data
- Expert opinions and benchmarks

#### Calculation Methods
- Number of target customers × average revenue per customer
- Number of transactions × average transaction value
- Observable proxies × expansion factors

### 3. Value Theory Approach

Estimate based on the value created and a reasonable share of that value.

#### Steps
- Quantify the value created per customer or transaction
- Determine a reasonable percentage of that value that could be captured
- Estimate the number of customers or transactions
- Calculate total market size

#### Value Quantification
- Cost savings or avoidance
- Revenue increase or acceleration
- Time saved and valued
- Risk reduction or mitigation
- Intangible benefits (converted to monetary value)

#### Capture Rate
- Based on business model and pricing
- Consider customer willingness to pay for value
- Look at analogous markets and solutions
- Typically ranges from 5% to 20% of value created

## Market Sizing Process

### 1. Preparation
- Clearly define the target market (who, what, where, when)
- Identify hypotheses about market size and structure
- Prepare market sizing templates
- Identify potential data sources for each approach

### 2. Top-Down Sizing

#### Broad Market Identification
- Identify a broad market with reliable data
- Document source and year of data

#### Segmentation and Filtering
- Apply each filter sequentially
- Document filter criteria and source
- Calculate remaining market after each filter

#### Calculation
- Final filtered market size
- Adjust for any known overlaps or double-counting

### 3. Bottom-Up Sizing

#### Unit Identification
- Define the observable unit (customer, transaction, etc.)
- Document source and method of identification

#### Unit Value Determination
- Determine value per unit (price, willingness to pay, etc.)
- Document source (customer interviews, tests, analogous data)

#### Market Unit Estimation
- Estimate total number of units in target market
- Document source and method (surveys, observable data, expert opinion)

#### Calculation
- Total units × value per unit

### 4. Value Theory Sizing

#### Value Quantification
- Quantify value created per unit (customer, transaction)
- Document sources and methods for value calculation

#### Capture Rate Estimation
- Estimate reasonable percentage of value that could be captured
- Document rationale and analogous examples

#### Market Unit Estimation
- Estimate total number of units in target market
- Document source and method

#### Calculation
- Total units × value per unit × capture rate

### 5. Triangulation and Validation

#### Compare Estimates
- Compare results from all three approaches
- Note similarities and significant differences

#### Investigate Discrepancies
- If estimates vary widely, re-examine assumptions and sources
- Look for errors in segmentation, unit definition, or value calculation

#### Final Estimate
- If estimates converge, use the average or median
- If a range, specify the low, most likely, and high estimates
- Document reasoning for final estimate

## Evidence Collection

- Record market definition (who, what, where, when)
- Document data sources with credibility assessments
- Capture assumptions and their sources for each approach
- Record calculations step by step for transparency
- Note any adjustments made for overlaps, double-counting, etc.
- Save examples of analogous markets or validation data

## Skill Overview

The market sizing skill enables practitioners to estimate the size of a venture's target market using multiple methodologies. Unlike relying on a single estimate or unfounded assumptions, this approach triangulates top-down, bottom-up, and value-theory methods to arrive at a more reliable market size range, providing critical insight into market attractiveness and venture potential.

## Key Differentiators

- Uses three distinct approaches: top-down, bottom-up, and value theory
- Requires explicit market definition (who, what, where, when)
- Grounds estimates in credible data sources and documented assumptions
- Triangulates estimates from different methods to validate and refine
- Documents calculations step by step for transparency and verification

## Common Applications

- Assessing market attractiveness before significant investment
- Informing financial projections and business models
- Supporting fundraising efforts with credible market estimates
- Guiding pricing, positioning, and go-to-market strategies
- Identifying market segments with the greatest potential

## Success Indicators

After applying this skill, you should be able to:
- Clearly define the target market (who, what, where, when)
- Conduct top-down market sizing using credible industry data
- Conduct bottom-up market sizing using observable customer data
- Conduct value theory market sizing based on value created
- Triangulate estimates from all three approaches
- Investigate and explain significant discrepancies between estimates
- Document a final market size estimate with clear reasoning

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Relying on a single market sizing approach without validation
- Using outdated or unreliable data sources
- Failing to define the target market clearly before sizing
- Making assumptions without documenting sources or rationale
- Not showing work or documenting calculations step by step
- Ignoring significant discrepancies between different approaches
- Overlooking overlaps or double-counting in segmentation
- Using capture rates without justification or analogous examples

## Next Steps

After completing market sizing, consider:
- Building financial models based on the market size estimate
- Testing pricing strategies through unit economics and LTV calculations
- Informing go-to-market strategies based on market characteristics
- Sharing market sizing with the venture team and advisors
