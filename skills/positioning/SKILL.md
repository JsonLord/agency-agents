# Positioning Skill

This skill provides a structured approach to defining and testing how a venture should be positioned in the minds of target customers relative to alternatives. Unlike vague marketing statements or feature comparisons, this skill emphasizes evidence-based positioning grounded in customer perceptions and validated differentiation.

## When to Use This Skill

- After value proposition development
- Before investing significantly in brand building or marketing campaigns
- When preparing to communicate with customers, investors, or partners
- To create a shared understanding of how we want to be perceived in the market

## Core Principles

1. **Position in the mind, not the marketplace** - Focus on customer perceptions, not just where we sell
2. **Start with the target customer** - Define position relative to who we're trying to reach
3. **Anchor to the customer problem** - Position around the problem we solve, not just what we sell
4. **Define relative to alternatives** - Position is meaningless without a frame of reference
5. **Focus on meaningful differentiation** - Only differences that matter to the customer count
6. **Test with target customers** - Validate that the position is actually held in the customer's mind

## Positioning Framework

A strong positioning statement answers four fundamental questions:

### 1. Who is it for? (Target Customer)
- Specific enough to be actionable
- Examples: "Small business owners with 5-50 employees," "Mobile app users who track fitness goals," "Restaurant owners struggling with no-shows"

### 2. What is the market? (Frame of Reference)
- What alternatives do customers currently consider?
- What category or context do they use to evaluate options?
- Examples: "Accessibility testing tools," "Fitness tracking apps," "Restaurant reservation systems"

### 3. What is the promise? (Key Benefit)
- The main benefit we want customers to associate with us
- Should be specific, measurable, and tied to the customer problem
- Examples: "Reduce accessibility fixing costs by 70%," "Increase workout consistency by 40%," "Save 5 hours per week on reservation management"

### 4. What is the reason to believe? (Proof Points)
- Evidence that supports our promise and differentiation
- Examples: "Validated in 100+ businesses," "Based on automated scanning technology," "Integrates with existing development workflows"

## Positioning Statement Format

For [target customer] who [struggle with/experience] [customer problem], [our solution] is the [frame of reference] that [key benefit] unlike [key alternative] by [unique mechanism] because [proof points].

## Examples

### Strong Positioning Statements
- "For small business owners who struggle with costly late-stage accessibility redesigns, our automated auditing tool is the accessibility testing solution that reduces fixing costs by 70% unlike manual audits by catching issues early in development because it integrates directly into CI/CD pipelines."
- "For mobile app users who struggle with staying motivated to exercise regularly, our social fitness challenge is the fitness tracking app that increases workout consistency by 40% unlike solo tracking apps by combining peer accountability with rewarding progression because it uses proven behavioral science principles."
- "For restaurant owners who struggle with time-consuming manual reservation processes, our online booking system is the restaurant reservation system that saves 5 hours per week on reservation management unlike paper books by automating confirmations, reminders, and table management because it reduces no-shows by 30% through automated reminders."

### Weak Positioning Statements (to avoid)
- "Our accessibility tool helps businesses with compliance." (Too vague, no differentiation)
- "We built a great fitness app with lots of features." (Feature-focused, not problem-positioned)
- "Our reservation system is better because we say so." (No customer validation or proof points)

## Positioning Development Process

### 1. Preparation
- Gather problem insights from customer research
- Review value proposition and solution benefits
- Identify target customer, problem, and alternatives
- Prepare positioning templates

### 2. Market Definition

#### Alternative Identification
- What do customers currently use to solve the problem?
- What do they consider when making a solution choice?
- What are the direct, indirect, and replacement alternatives?

#### Category Determination
- How do customers categorize solutions to this problem?
- What terms do they use when talking about solution types?
- What context do they use when evaluating options?

#### Competitive Landscape
- Who are the main players in this space?
- What are their strengths and weaknesses?
- What market share or customer perception do they have?

### 3. Problem Anchoring

#### Problem Validation
- Confirm the problem is real, painful, and worth solving
- Quantify problem frequency, intensity, and impact when possible
- Identify which aspects of the problem our solution addresses

#### Problem Language
- What language do customers use to describe the problem?
- What emotions, frustrations, and urgencies do they associate with it?
- How do they talk about the problem when seeking solutions?

### 4. Benefit Articulation

#### Solution Benefits
- Identify what our solution actually does for the customer
- Determine which benefits are most valuable to the customer
- Validate benefits through prototyping, testing, or customer feedback

#### Benefit Quantification
- Convert solution benefits into concrete metrics whenever possible
- Use customer language to describe the benefit
- Consider both financial and non-financial benefits (time, stress, quality, etc.)

### 5. Differentiation Definition

#### Alternative Analysis
- Identify what customers currently do to solve the problem
- Undercome the strengths and weaknesses of current alternatives
- Determine how our solution is different and preferably better

#### Meaningful Differences
- Focus on differences that matter to the customer (price, quality, time, etc.)
- Avoid meaningless differences ("we use blue buttons" vs "they use red")

#### Unique Mechanism
- Explain how we deliver the benefit in a way that alternatives don't
- This is often the "secret sauce" or core innovation

### 6. Proof Points Development

#### Validation Evidence
- What evidence supports our claimed benefits?
- What evidence shows our differentiation is real?
- What evidence shows we're better than alternatives?

#### Credibility Builders
- What makes customers believe we can deliver what we promise?
- What shows our solution is reliable and trustworthy?
- What demonstrates our expertise and commitment to the problem?

### 7. Positioning Testing

#### Comprehension Testing
- Do target customers understand our positioning statement?
- Do they understand who we're for, what we do, and how we're different?

#### Resonance Testing
- Does the positioning statement feel relevant and compelling to target customers?
- Does it address something they actually care about?

#### Credibility Testing
- Do target customers believe we can deliver what we promise?
- Does the position align with what they know about us and alternatives?

#### Competitive Testing
- How does our position compare to alternatives in the eyes of customers?
- Would they choose our solution over alternatives based on the positioning statement alone?

#### Memory Testing
- Can target customers recall our positioning after a delay?
- Does our positioning stick in their mind better than alternatives?

### 8. Positioning Refinement

#### Iterative Improvement
- Test, learn, and refine based on customer feedback
- Try different formulations to see what resonates best
- Consider segment-specific positioning for different customer types

#### Consistency Check
- Ensure the positioning aligns with our problem insights, value proposition, and differentiation
- Verify that claimed benefits and differentiation are actually supported by evidence

## Evidence Collection

- Record the target customer and problem being addressed
- Document the solution benefits and how they were validated
- Capture the alternatives analysis and differentiation points
- Record proof points and credibility builders
- Collect customer feedback on comprehension, resonance, credibility, competitiveness, and memory

## Skill Overview

The positioning skill enables practitioners to create and test positioning statements that define how a venture should be perceived in the minds of target customers relative to alternatives. Unlike marketing slogans or feature comparisons, this approach emphasizes evidence-based positioning grounded in customer perceptions and validated differentiation, ensuring that the position actually exists in the customer's mind and provides a competitive advantage.

## Key Differentiators

- Focuses on starting with target customer and their problem rather than solution features
- Requires positioning statements to be specific about target customer, problem, market, benefit, and differentiation
- Demands clear differentiation from alternatives that matters to the customer
- Requires proof points that support the claimed benefits and differentiation
- Mandates testing with target customers before finalizing the positioning statement

## Common Applications

- Developing positioning for new ventures and products
- Refining positioning based on customer feedback and validation
- Creating investor-ready positioning statements
- Building sales and marketing materials around customer perception
- Communicating why customers should choose our solution over alternatives

## Success Indicators

After applying this skill, you should be able to:
- Articulate positioning statements that start with specific target customer and problem
- Identify specific, validated target customers and their problems
- Specify specific, relevant frame of reference (market or category)
- Specify specific, measurable key benefit tied to customer problem
- Clearly differentiate our solution from alternatives in meaningful ways
- Provide proof points that support the key benefit and differentiation
- Test positioning statements with target customers and refine based on feedback
- Use customer language rather than jargon in positioning statements

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Starting with solution features rather than customer problems
- Being vague about the target customer or their problem
- Failing to validate solution benefits through customer feedback
- Creating undifferentiated positioning statements that don't stand out in the customer's mind
- Using jargon, acronyms, or unclear language that hinders understanding
- Finalizing positioning statements without testing with target customers

## Next Steps

After completing positioning development, consider:
- Creating pricing strategies aligned with the positioning and value proposition
- Designing offers that operationalize the positioning and value proposition
- Building minimum viable products that deliver the promised positioning
- Sharing positioning statements with the venture team and advisors
